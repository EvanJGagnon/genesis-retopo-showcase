How to read these

Genesis works part by part. It rebuilds the parts it can prove it improved and preserves the rest byte for byte, so a result is usually a mix: new topology where it earned it, original geometry everywhere else. The “rebuilt” figure counts only triangles Genesis generated — carried-through geometry gets no credit.

That is why the reduction ratios look modest next to the rebuilt counts. The compressor drops only 1.27× overall because 37 of its 61 selected parts failed a quality gate and were kept exactly as they were. Genesis would rather hand back your original part than a worse one.

Smoothing is doing some of the work

The flat-shaded row is deliberately unflattering: it lights every polygon separately, so a reduced curve looks like cut glass. The smooth row is the same geometry with angle-aware shading, which is how the asset would actually be used. On the compressor tank the difference is dramatic — most of the apparent damage is shading, not shape. Worth knowing before judging the geometry.

What every result passed

No self-intersections. Genus and part count preserved. Consistent normals, no degenerate faces, silhouette and surface fidelity inside the frozen tolerances. The source objects came back untouched in all six cases, verified by hash rather than by eye.

The honest limitation

These are 1.3–2.5× reductions. A per-part safety clamp currently stops Genesis rebuilding any part below 40% of its original density, so the aggressive case — a 60k prop down to 5k — is outside what the current architecture will attempt. That ceiling is the next thing on the roadmap, not a property of the approach.
